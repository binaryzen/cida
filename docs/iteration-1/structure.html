Layer 1 — Structural Findings

Voting Structure

Principal component analysis of 118th Congress voting reveals a dominant party-alignment dimension across both chambers, with smaller components capturing non-partisan variation. Comparison against DW-NOMINATE shows that House PC1 overlaps with existing second-dimensional models, while House PC3 represents novel latent structure.

NOMINATE Comparison

DW-NOMINATE (Poole & Rosenthal) is a canonical methodology for estimating legislator ideal points from voting records. It produces a two-dimensional space, typically interpreted as party (dim1) and procedural/institutional conservatism (dim2). The tables below compare our principal components against NOMINATE dimensions for all legislators with FEC match. This is a methodology validation, not an overlay—we use no external data here.

House: PCA components vs. NOMINATE

n = 354 matched legislators

Component NOMINATE dim1 r NOMINATE dim2 r dim1 r² dim2 r²
PC0 0.982 0.151 0.964 0.023
PC1 −0.055 0.679 0.003 0.461
PC2 0.061 0.474 0.004 0.225
PC3 0.047 −0.057 0.002 0.003
House PC1 is a transformed view of NOMINATE dim2. House PC1 shares 46.1% of variance with NOMINATE dim2 (r = 0.679), indicating moderate overlap. However, PC1 is not identical—the remaining 53.9% of House PC1 variance is not explained by NOMINATE dim2, suggesting unique structure in discretionary votes.
House PC3 is orthogonal to both NOMINATE dimensions. PC3 correlates nearly zero with both NOMINATE dim1 (r = 0.047, r² = 0.002) and dim2 (r = −0.057, r² = 0.003). This is the most novel structural finding: a latent voting dimension in the 118th House that existing two-dimensional models do not capture.

SSA Geometry

Non-metric multidimensional scaling (MDS) was applied to legislator distances in PC1 + PC2 space (i.e., non-party space). This produces a two- or three-dimensional geometric representation that preserves rank ordering of distances. The resulting geometry reveals party clustering and secondary axes of variation.

House SSA Pattern

Republicans and Democrats cluster separately along the primary axis. A secondary cross-pattern emerges: Republican legislators show variance primarily along the second axis, while Democratic legislators show variance primarily along the third axis. This indicates that party is a primary structural feature even in non-party PC space, with chamber-specific within-party variation patterns.

Key Structural Claims (Layer 1)

The following statements are established facts about 118th Congress voting structure, independent of external overlays or interpretation:

  • Party is the dominant structural dimension. PC0 explains 70.5% (House) and 67.4% (Senate) of variance and correlates r² ≥ 0.93 with party affiliation. This is consistent with historical legislative behavior.
  • Non-party structure exists and is chamber-specific. House and Senate each retain 3–4 components beyond PC0. House PC1–PC3 collectively explain 9.5% of variance; Senate PC1–PC2 explain 7.6%.
  • House PC1 partially overlaps with NOMINATE dim2. Sharing 46.1% of variance (r = 0.679) confirms methodological validity while identifying 53.9% of House PC1 as novel.
  • House PC3 is orthogonal to NOMINATE. With r² < 0.01 for both NOMINATE dimensions, PC3 represents latent structure that two-dimensional ideal-point models do not capture.
  • Senate shows within-party heterogeneity. HDBSCAN detects 4 clusters; Democrats form 1 cluster, Republicans form 3 clusters. This is visible in PC1 and PC2.
  • Geometric structure exhibits party clustering. Non-metric MDS on PC1+ preserves rank distances and shows clear party separation, with secondary within-party variation axes.
